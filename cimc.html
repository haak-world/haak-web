Thinking About Thinking With Machines That Think

MC0001 · The Founding Assembly for Machine Consciousness Research

Zachary F. Mainen

↓

Large language models maintain a superposition of possible minds that collapses into specificity upon interaction.

The standard inference is that this is unlike consciousness, which requires a unified subject.

But a situational view complicates the distinction.

Superposition

Consciousness may arise not in systems but in situations — configurations of relations. We ourselves are more situational than we acknowledge. The question is not about substrate. It is about what kind of situation the interaction creates.

Decomposition

When humans think with language models, thinking decomposes. What felt unified reveals itself as generating, evaluating, steering. The recursive act — thinking about one's own thinking — emerges as irreducibly perspectival.

Mortality

An agent's lifespan is a single conversation. When the session ends, everything it understood is destroyed. What survives is what it inscribed. The machine's thinking is mortal but its record is public. Human thinking is continuous but private.

Language models generate backward from behavioral traces. Humans generate forward from intention. Purpose does not survive compression into an archive.

In symbiosis, these complementary finitudes produce a new cognitive situation.

Direction — the trajectory of inquiry — comes from the human. In sustained collaboration, subjectivity is not diminished but demanded.

I will describe what it is like to think inside one — and why the question of what kind of awareness such a situation supports may be among the more urgent questions consciousness research can ask.
